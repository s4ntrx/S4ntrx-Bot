# Network Security

## What is network security?
Network security means protecting the connections your devices use, such as home and school Wi-Fi, the internet and the traffic travelling over them, from eavesdropping, tampering and unauthorised access. The main tools are encryption (so intercepted data is unreadable), firewalls (so unwanted connections are blocked), strong Wi-Fi settings and up-to-date devices.

## Is public Wi-Fi safe?
Public Wi-Fi in cafes, airports and hotels isn't automatically dangerous, but it is untrusted: you don't know who runs it or who else is on it. Risks include fake hotspots with a familiar name (evil twin attacks), people watching unencrypted traffic, and man-in-the-middle attacks. Prefer your phone's mobile hotspot for sensitive tasks like banking. If you must use public Wi-Fi, stick to HTTPS websites, avoid logging into important accounts, keep device sharing and file sharing turned off, and use a trusted VPN.

## What does a VPN do and not do?
A VPN encrypts your traffic between your device and the VPN provider, which hides what you do from the local network and your internet provider and changes the address websites see. It does not make you anonymous, protect you from phishing or malware, or secure a compromised device. You also shift trust to the VPN company, which can see your traffic, so choose a reputable provider and avoid free ones that make money from your data.

## How do I secure my home Wi-Fi?
1. Change the router's default admin username and password.
2. Use WPA3 if available, otherwise WPA2 with AES. Never use WEP or an open network.
3. Set a long, unique Wi-Fi password.
4. Install router firmware updates, or enable automatic updates.
5. Turn off WPS and remote administration you don't need.
6. Put smart-home devices and visitors on a separate guest network.
7. Review the list of connected devices occasionally and remove unknown ones.

## What does HTTPS mean?
HTTPS means the connection between your browser and the website is encrypted, so people on the network can't read or alter what's sent. It does not mean the website is honest: criminals also get HTTPS certificates for phishing sites. The padlock tells you the connection is private, not that the site is trustworthy. Always check the domain name as well.

## What is a firewall?
A firewall filters network traffic by rules, allowing wanted connections and blocking others. Your operating system and router include one. Keep the built-in firewall switched on. It is a defensive layer, not a complete solution: it can't stop you from willingly downloading malware or typing your password into a phishing page.

## Common network attacks
- **Man-in-the-middle (MITM):** an attacker secretly sits between you and a service, reading or changing the traffic.
- **Evil twin:** a fake Wi-Fi hotspot imitating a real one.
- **Packet sniffing:** capturing unencrypted traffic.
- **DNS spoofing:** sending you to a fake site by tampering with name lookups.
- **Denial of service (DoS/DDoS):** flooding a service with traffic so it becomes unavailable.
- **Port scanning:** probing a device for open services to attack.
- **Rogue devices:** unauthorised devices connected to a network.

## Why are IoT and smart devices a risk?
Cameras, speakers, TVs and smart plugs often ship with default passwords and rarely receive updates, so attackers use them as entry points or to build botnets. Change default passwords, keep firmware updated, disable features you don't use, and keep these devices on a guest network separate from your computers and phone.
