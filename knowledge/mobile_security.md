# Mobile Device Security

## How do I keep my phone secure?
Your phone holds your messages, photos, email, banking and the multi-factor codes for your other accounts, so securing it protects everything else. The basics are a strong screen lock, automatic updates, apps from official stores only, careful permissions, encrypted backups and a way to find or wipe it if it's lost. Most phones encrypt storage by default once a screen lock is set.

## What screen lock should I use?
Use a PIN of at least six digits, or a password, and add fingerprint or face unlock for convenience. Avoid easy patterns, birthdays or 1234. Set the screen to lock automatically after a short time and hide notification content on the lock screen so codes and messages aren't visible to people nearby. Never share your PIN, and be aware of shoulder surfing in public.

## Why do I need to update my phone?
Updates fix security flaws that attackers actively exploit. Turn on automatic updates for the operating system and apps. If your phone no longer receives security updates from the manufacturer, it is increasingly unsafe for banking and important accounts, and replacing it is worth considering.

## Are apps from outside the official store safe?
Official stores check apps, though some malicious ones still get through. Apps installed from links, messages or unofficial sites (sideloading) are much riskier, especially "free" versions of paid apps and modified game apps. Before installing, check the publisher, reviews, download count and the permissions requested. Delete apps you don't use.

## What is a SIM swap attack and how do I prevent it?
A SIM swap is when a criminal convinces your mobile carrier to move your number to their SIM, letting them receive your calls and text codes. Protect yourself by adding a PIN or security lock to your carrier account, using an authenticator app or passkey instead of SMS codes where possible, and keeping your number private. If your phone suddenly loses signal unexpectedly, contact your carrier quickly.

## What should I do if my phone is lost or stolen?
Use Find My Device (Android) or Find My iPhone to locate, lock or erase it. Change passwords for important accounts, especially email, and sign out of sessions. Contact your carrier to block the SIM, and report the loss to your school and, for theft, the police. Regular encrypted backups mean you can restore your data to a new phone.

## What are the risks of public charging and Bluetooth?
Plugging into unknown public USB ports can in rare cases expose a device to attack, so carry your own charger or cable, or use a power-only adapter, and choose "charge only" if the phone asks about data access. Turn off Bluetooth, AirDrop or nearby sharing when you don't need them and don't accept files from people you don't know.

## How can I recognise mobile scams and stalkerware?
Common scams include texts about parcels, banking alerts, fake "sent by mistake" mobile-money messages and prize notifications. Don't tap links in unexpected texts. Stalkerware is an app that secretly tracks a phone. Warning signs include unexplained battery drain, unknown apps or device administrator settings, and someone knowing things they shouldn't. If you suspect it, tell a trusted person or your school's IT contact first, because removing it may alert the person who installed it.
