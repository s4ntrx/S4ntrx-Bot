# Safe Browsing

## What is safe browsing?
Safe browsing means using the web in ways that avoid scams, malicious sites and tracking. The core habits are keeping your browser updated, checking where a link really goes, downloading only from official sources, being careful with extensions and pop-ups, and never entering passwords on a page you reached through a message.

## How do I read a web address (URL) correctly?
A URL looks like https://login.example.com/account?id=7. The part that matters is the registered domain, which is the last two parts before the first single slash (here example.com). Everything to the left, like "login", is a subdomain anyone who owns the domain can choose. So in https://paypal.com.secure-check.net/login the real site is secure-check.net, not PayPal. Read the domain from right to left, and be suspicious of long addresses, extra hyphens and unfamiliar endings.

## What is typosquatting and a lookalike domain?
Attackers register addresses that look nearly identical to real ones, like gooogle.com, arnazon.com (rn instead of m) or a zero in place of the letter o. Some use lookalike characters from other alphabets (homograph attacks). Always type important addresses yourself or use bookmarks, and check the domain carefully before logging in.

## Does the padlock or HTTPS mean a site is safe?
No. The padlock means your connection to the site is encrypted, not that the site is honest. Phishing sites commonly use HTTPS. Judge a site by its domain, how you got there and whether it asks for sensitive information, not by the padlock alone.

## How do I download files safely?
Download software only from the publisher's official site or an official app store. Avoid cracked software, game cheats and "free" paid programs: they are one of the most common ways malware spreads. Check file names for double extensions like invoice.pdf.exe. Be wary of files that ask you to enable macros or run a script. If you're unsure, scan the file or ask IT before opening it.

## What are fake virus warnings and tech-support scams?
Pop-ups claiming "Your computer is infected, call this number" are scams. Real websites can't scan your device from a browser tab. Don't call the number, don't click inside the pop-up and don't allow remote access. Close the tab or quit the browser, and if it won't close, restart the browser. Then check your installed extensions and run a normal security scan.

## Are browser extensions safe?
Extensions can read what you do on websites, so a malicious or compromised one can steal passwords and browsing data. Install as few as possible, only from the official store, check the publisher, reviews and the permissions requested, and remove extensions you no longer use. Be suspicious of extensions that ask for access to "all websites" without a clear reason.

## How can I tell if a website is fake or a scam?
Search results and social media ads can lead to fake shops and fake login pages, so for banking and important accounts use bookmarks. Be wary of deals that seem too good to be true, shops with no contact details and sites that only accept bank transfers or gift cards. Shortened links hide destinations, so use a link preview tool, or paste the link into the URL Analyzer here. Scan QR codes only from sources you trust and check the address it opens.

## What about cookies, trackers and private browsing?
Cookies keep you logged in and remember settings, but trackers also follow you across sites to build a profile. You can limit tracking by clearing cookies, blocking third-party cookies and reviewing site permissions. Private or incognito mode only avoids saving history on your device; it doesn't hide you from websites, your school or your internet provider. Keep your browser updated, and log out of shared computers.
