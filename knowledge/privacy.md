# Privacy and Personal Data

## What is personal data and why does it matter?
Personal data is information that identifies you or can be linked to you: your name, student ID, address, phone number, email, birthday, photos, location, account details and even habits. Criminals use it for identity theft, to guess security answers, to impersonate you and to make phishing messages convincing. Sharing less gives attackers less to work with.

## What is a digital footprint?
Your digital footprint is everything about you that exists online: what you post, what others post about you, accounts you've created and data collected from your activity. Much of it is permanent, because screenshots and copies exist even after you delete a post. Before you share something, ask whether you'd be comfortable with a future employer, a stranger or an attacker seeing it.

## What shouldn't I share publicly?
Avoid posting your full birthday, home address, school timetable, phone number, student ID, travel plans while you're away, photos of tickets, ID cards, boarding passes or keys, and anything that reveals passwords or answers to security questions. Photos can also contain location data (geotags), so turn location tagging off in your camera settings before posting.

## How do I make my social media safer?
Set accounts to private where possible and review who can see posts, tag you or message you. Accept friend requests only from people you actually know, since fake profiles are used for scams and information gathering. Turn on multi-factor authentication, review connected apps and logins, and check your tag and visibility settings regularly. Report and block accounts that harass or pressure you.

## What should I do after a data breach?
1. Find out which service was affected and what data was exposed.
2. Change the password for that account straight away, and for any other account using the same password.
3. Turn on multi-factor authentication.
4. Watch for phishing that uses the leaked details.
5. If financial information was exposed, contact your bank.
6. Check your email address on Have I Been Pwned to learn about other breaches.

## What are app permissions?
Apps ask for access to your camera, microphone, contacts, location, photos and storage. Grant only what an app genuinely needs: a calculator doesn't need your contacts. Review permissions in your phone settings, choose "only while using the app" for location, and remove apps you no longer use. Be careful with apps and websites that ask you to hand over lots of data in return for something free.

## How does information about me help attackers?
Attackers gather public details from social media, school pages and data leaks to build a convincing story, a practice called open-source intelligence (OSINT). They may know your teachers, your hobbies and your friends, which makes a phishing message or a scam call far more believable. Reducing what is public makes you a harder target.

## How should I handle other people's data?
Don't share other people's photos, messages or personal details without permission, and don't forward private information. Be careful when sharing screenshots, since they may show names, numbers or private messages. Respecting privacy protects friends and classmates, and it is a legal obligation in many places under data protection laws.
