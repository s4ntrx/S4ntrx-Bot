# Social Engineering

## Definition
Social engineering is manipulating people — rather than exploiting
technical vulnerabilities — into breaking normal security procedures or
revealing confidential information.

## Common tactics
- **Pretexting** — inventing a fabricated scenario (e.g., posing as IT support) to obtain information.
- **Baiting** — leaving infected USB drives or offering "free" downloads to lure victims.
- **Tailgating** — following an authorized person into a restricted area without their own credentials.
- **Vishing** — voice-call phishing, often impersonating a bank or tech support.
- **Quid pro quo** — offering a service or benefit in exchange for information or access.

## Defenses
- Verify identity through a separate channel before acting on unusual requests.
- Be skeptical of urgency and authority pressure — attackers rely on you
  not stopping to think.
- Follow your school/organization's verification procedures even if the
  requester claims to be in a hurry or "important."
- Report suspicious contact attempts, even if you didn't fall for them —
  it helps others recognize the same attempt.
